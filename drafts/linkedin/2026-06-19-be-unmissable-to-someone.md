Most founders think their job is to be wanted by everyone.

It's the opposite. Your job is to be unmissable to someone, and a polite no to everyone else.

I was with a room of founders in Malaysia this morning, and we kept circling back to the same truth. You don't pick your customer. You watch who actually says yes, then you have the discipline to drop the rest.

A founder I mentored had a product that could help almost any small business, so she sold to all of them, slowly. We listed her happiest paying customers and the pattern was unmissable. Clinic owners who had just hired their first admin staff. She rebuilt around that one person and hit profit fast.

A few things I keep coming back to:

Watch what people do, not what they say. "I'd definitely buy that" in a survey is worth nothing. A signature, a renewal, a referral is worth everything.

Speed is the tell. The right fit buys quickly, without a six-month internal war. Slow, painful deals are usually a fit problem wearing a process costume.

The referral is the strongest signal of all. If a happy customer can send you someone who looks just like them, you've found your someone.

"Our product is for everyone" is the most expensive sentence in a startup. It sounds like ambition. It behaves like a millstone, and it sends investors running for the hills.

Name the one segment you'll stop chasing, and say it out loud. That's the part founders flinch at, and it's the one that frees the time and budget for the people who actually fit.

Stop trying to be wanted by everyone. Go and be unmissable to someone.
