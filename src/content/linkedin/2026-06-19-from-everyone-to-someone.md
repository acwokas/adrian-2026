The most expensive sentence I hear in a startup is four words long: our product is for everyone.

It sounds like ambition. It behaves like fog.

This morning I ran a fireside chat with founders in Malaysia, and the same truth landed in the first ten minutes. Most founders think their job is to be wanted by everyone. It is the opposite. Your job is to be unmissable to someone, and a polite no to everyone else.

A few things I keep coming back to:

You do not pick your customer. You notice which customers are picking you, then have the discipline to drop the rest.

Watch what they do, not what they say. A signature, a renewal, a referral beats any survey.

Speed is the tell. The right fit buys fast. Slow, painful deals are usually a fit problem wearing a process costume.

A niche is a beachhead, not a prison. A sharp message travels. A generic one gets ignored.

When I started AIinASIA I tried to write for everyone, then narrowed to the operators in Asia who actually adopt this stuff. The generic pieces sat there. The focused ones travelled, and now about ten thousand people read it every month with no paid distribution.

So stop trying to be wanted by everyone. Go and be unmissable to someone. The rest of your strategy gets easier the moment you choose.
