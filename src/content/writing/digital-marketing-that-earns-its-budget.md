Digital marketing earns its budget through a credible connection between the money spent, the customer response and the value created.

That connection takes work. A campaign can generate attention while sales remain flat. A useful article can influence a buying decision weeks before an enquiry arrives. A dashboard can allocate revenue to a channel without showing how much additional business the activity created.

Commercial judgement begins by being precise about what the evidence can support.

## Start with the customer decision

Before choosing a channel, identify the decision you want to help a customer make.

Consider a hypothetical business selling a complex service. A prospective customer may already understand the problem but feel uncertain about implementation. Another awareness campaign might attract visitors while leaving that uncertainty untouched.

A clear implementation guide, a relevant case study or a conversation with a specialist could address the actual obstacle.

The marketing brief should describe the audience, the decision, the barrier and the next useful action. That makes creative work easier to evaluate. It also gives sales and delivery teams a meaningful role in shaping the campaign.

## Separate measurement from proof

Measurement needs definitions that everyone involved can understand.

[Google Analytics describes attribution](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10596866?hl=en) as allocating credit for important actions across interactions along a customer's path. That allocation can help explain recorded journeys. It does not, by itself, establish how many sales would have happened without the marketing. Allocated credit and additional sales answer different questions.

Imagine a hypothetical campaign report showing that paid search received credit for several purchases. Those purchases matter. The next questions concern customer intent, existing demand, profitability and whether the campaign reached people who would otherwise have bought anyway.

I would want a report to separate observed results, attributed results and remaining uncertainty. Where practical, comparisons or controlled tests can strengthen the commercial assessment. The method should suit the decision and the budget available.

## Fund the next decision

Suppose a hypothetical campaign generates plenty of enquiries, but few become qualified opportunities. Increasing spend immediately would repeat an unresolved problem.

The next investment might instead improve targeting, clarify the offer or examine how enquiries are handled. Each option addresses a different explanation. The team should agree which explanation it is testing and what evidence would support continuing.

This also means including the cost of follow-up and delivery. A campaign that attracts expensive, poorly matched enquiries can consume sales capacity even when the reported cost per lead looks attractive.

The discipline is to connect activity with the full commercial journey: attention, meaningful interest, a buying decision and value delivered to the customer.

Marketing deserves room for creativity and experimentation. Clear commercial questions make that room easier to protect. They allow teams to explain what they learned, where confidence has increased and why the next pound should go to a particular activity.
