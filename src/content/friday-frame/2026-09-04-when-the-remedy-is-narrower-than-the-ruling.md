---
title: "When the remedy is narrower than the ruling."
date: 2026-09-04
summary: "A court can find an illegal monopoly and still leave the two sides of the trade under one owner. The ruling landed this week; the actual rules won't be public for another month."
---

A CMO called this week wanting to know what Wednesday's Google ad tech ruling changes for her media plan. The honest answer took longer to land than I expected, because the ruling itself is only half public.

Judge Brinkema found Google's ad tech stack an illegal monopoly back in April 2025. This week she ruled on the remedy, and turned down every structural fix the Department of Justice asked for: no forced sale of the AdX exchange, no opening up the auction logic locked inside DFP, no split-off of what's left. Instead she accepted most of the behavioural remedies both sides had proposed, reshaped where she saw fit. What those remedies actually require isn't public yet. Redactions are due 16 September; the final judgment isn't due until 2 October. Nobody buying media through that stack knows the operative rules.

That's the part worth sitting with. A court can name a monopoly and still leave both sides of the trade under one owner, a tighter referee rather than a different market. Waiting on this ruling to settle your adtech concentration risk was never quite the right posture, and a sealed month ahead makes that plain. The stack you're buying through today is the stack you'll be buying through in October, plus some auction rules nobody's read yet.

If your operating model was banking on a court to redesign the market for you, October's judgment won't do that either. Someone inside your organisation should already own that exposure.
